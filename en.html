1. Business Overview

1.1 Background

The Korea tour business connects Envoy’s on-the-ground tour operating capability in Georgia and Armenia with Korean customers and travel agency partners.

Envoy has operated hostels and tours in Georgia and Armenia for around 20 years and has an established operating base in Tbilisi and Yerevan. Its ability to manage accommodation, guides, transport, restaurants and local suppliers directly, and to respond to issues on the ground, is an important asset for the Korean market.

Within the wider Envoy brand, tours act as a gateway for customers to discover the Caucasus through Georgia and Armenia. Rather than simply taking customers to tourist sites, Envoy aims to connect local history, culture, nature, food and people so that customers can experience the region more deeply.

The Korea business will operate with a lean structure. In Korea, the focus will be on understanding the market and customer needs, co-developing and localising products, developing travel agency partners, marketing, sales and generating customer enquiries.

Travel contracts and payments will be handled through licensed travel agency partners. The Georgia and Armenia tour operations will be responsible for product co-development, costing, supplier management and booking, guide, transport, accommodation and restaurant management, and actual tour operations.

Envoy Tour Korea may be used as the external business brand in the Korean market.

1.2 Current Operating Base

The Korea operation will be led by one person. No additional full-time staff will be added until the business proves itself through actual bookings and revenue. Resources will remain focused on product development and localisation, partner management, marketing and sales.

Marine manages overall tour operations in Armenia, while local guides operate in Georgia. The Georgia BAM team may also support communication with early Korean groups and on-site operations where needed.

2. Market and External Environment

2.1 Korean Outbound Travel Market

Korean outbound travel demand has largely recovered since COVID-19, but high exchange rates, long-haul airfares and geopolitical uncertainty have made customers more price-sensitive and cautious.

For relatively unfamiliar and distant destinations such as Georgia and Armenia, customers do not make purchase decisions based on destination appeal alone. They also compare total trip cost, flight options, itinerary density, accommodation quality, guides and transport, local operator reliability and safety conditions.

In the Korean market, it is therefore important to communicate clearly why customers should choose this region and why they should travel with Envoy.

2.2 Travel Consumer Trends in Korea

Travellers increasingly value experiences that match their interests and preferences, including nature, food, local culture, accommodation and interaction with people, rather than simply visiting as many famous sites as possible.

Mountain travel is also expanding beyond specialist hiking and trekking toward staying in scenic places, taking light walks and experiencing local food and culture. This creates an opportunity to use Georgia’s Caucasus mountain environment, wine and food, smaller communities, as well as Armenia’s cultural heritage and local people.

However, the existence of these travel trends does not in itself prove product competitiveness. We still need to validate whether customers have a strong enough reason to pay for an Envoy product instead of choosing an existing package or travelling independently.

Envoy’s Accessible Boutique direction is not about unnecessary luxury. It means concentrating spend on the parts of the journey customers genuinely feel: accommodation, food, guides and local experiences.

2.3 Georgia/Caucasus Product Market and Competition

Several major and specialist Korean travel companies already sell Georgia and Caucasus packages. Existing products already include combinations of Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan, premium hotels, wine and local food, nature, hiking and no-shopping itineraries.

Competing mainly on the number of attractions, hotel grade or price is therefore not suited to Envoy’s current scale or strengths.

  • A clear purpose and story for the journey
  • Experiences made possible by a small group
  • Places, meals, accommodation and programmes selected locally
  • Connection with local people
  • Detailed operation tailored to Korean customers
  • Direct operating capability in Georgia and Armenia

2.4 Christian Travel Market and Competing Products

Korea already has a broad pilgrimage market, including Israel and Türkiye, and some products also cover Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan or Georgia and Armenia together. The number of countries or religious sites visited is therefore not enough to differentiate the product.

Envoy’s Christian Heritage Tour is structured as one connected story, beginning with St. Nino and the Christianisation of Georgia, then continuing through Gregory the Illuminator, the Christianisation of Armenia and Mount Ararat. Key differentiators include the Geghard vocal experience, interpretation tailored to Korean Protestant customers, and a balance of Christian heritage with nature and culture in places such as Kazbegi and Kakheti.

Whether these product differences can actually generate groups of 14 or more will be tested through sales activity in 2026 and the first departure in 2027.

2.5 Air Access, Travel Advisories and External Environment

Caucasus products are highly affected by air access, international developments and customer perceptions of travel safety.

There is still no scheduled nonstop service between Korea and Georgia, and nonstop access depends on limited charter flights. According to the Georgian Civil Aviation Agency, only 11 charter flights operated between 2019 and 2025. T’way Air’s planned Incheon–Tbilisi/Yerevan charters for 2026 were cancelled in both the first and second half of the year. Air access remains one of the largest constraints on expanding the Korean market.

According to the Republic of Korea Ministry of Foreign Affairs, most of Georgia is under Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution / Travel with Caution), while South Ossetia and Abkhazia are under Level 3 (Recommendation to Leave / Avoid Travel). Envoy’s main tour areas are not in the Level 3 zones, but the Level 2 advisory applying to most of the country may still influence some Korean customers and travel agency partners.

2.6 Current Reading of the Korea–Georgia Travel Market

Korean visits to Georgia grew quickly over the last few years. According to the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Georgia, Korean visitors increased from around 14,000 in 2023 to around 26,000 in 2024 and exceeded 31,000 in 2025.

This shows a clear growth in Korean awareness and interest in Georgia through 2025. However, the absolute market remains small, and the lack of scheduled nonstop flights creates a major constraint in converting interest into actual bookings and group travel.

3. Business Model

3.1 Role and Structure of the Korea Business

In Korea, the role is to develop the market and customers and to turn Envoy’s local tour services into products suitable for Korean customers.

  • Understand the Korean market and customer needs
  • Co-develop and localise products with the local team
  • Develop and manage travel agency partners
  • Operate Korean-language product pages and social media
  • Sell to churches, organisations and group leaders
  • Generate customer enquiries and connect them with partner travel agencies
  • Manage product-level revenue and settlement

Partner travel agencies handle customer recruitment, travel contracts and payments, while the Georgia and Armenia operations supply and operate the travel services.

3.2 Korea–Georgia–Armenia Operating Structure

Both core products will be standardised where practical.

The Christian Heritage Tour will have a standard itinerary, core narrative and key programmes. The Boutique Journey will keep its 9-night/10-day route and signature experiences while allowing limited adjustments to accommodation level, food emphasis, walking intensity, selected programmes and free time depending on the group.

Once a booking is confirmed, the local team will finalise accommodation, vehicles, guides, restaurants, sites and programmes. After the trip, Korea and the local team will review actual COGS, customer feedback and operating issues and apply the lessons to future departures.

3.3 Roles of Each Entity and Settlement Structure

PartyMain Role
Korean partner travel agencyCustomer travel contract, collection of travel payments, consumer protection and travel agency legal responsibilities, and setting the final retail price including airfare
Local Envoy tour entityLocal operator: contracts with hotels, transport, guides, restaurants and programme providers; manages local COGS and actual tour operations
Korea entityKorean market development, product co-development and localisation, marketing, sales to group leaders and travel agencies, partner management and lead generation

Supply prices are set by adding a target GP to local COGS. The initial internal allocation principle is 50:50 between the Korean market development/sales function and the local operating function.

Customer travel payments are collected through licensed travel agencies. The Korea entity does not contract directly with customers or receive customer travel payments. The Korea entity receives its share for Korean market development, marketing and sales. The detailed contractual structure, Korea–Georgia remittance process and tax treatment will be finalised after accounting and tax review.

3.4 Operating Principles

Through 2028, the priority is to maintain a lean and efficient structure rather than register a standalone travel agency or expand the organisation.

The business will make maximum use of partner travel agencies’ sales and contracting functions and Envoy’s existing local operation, minimising new fixed costs in Korea.

Confirmed booking → Actual departure → Customer satisfaction → Repeat sales → Profitability

Until performance is proven, investment in additional staff, office space and standalone systems will be kept to a minimum.

3.5 Envoy F&B Integration

Common Ground Café and the rooftop café will be naturally integrated into the tour itinerary to create an experience that is distinctly Envoy.

For the Boutique Journey in particular, elements such as the Old Tbilisi Envoy Welcome, hand-drip coffee experience and Welcome Dinner at the rooftop café will connect the beginning of the trip with the Envoy brand experience.

4.2.5 Retail Price Positioning

The Christian Heritage supply price is based on land cost. The final retail price will include airfare and the partner travel agency’s selling margin. Based on current competing products and recent long-haul airfares, the target retail price is approximately KRW 4.7–4.9 million.

Price itself will not be the main competitive factor. The value needs to come from the 14-person small-group format, 11-night/12-day itinerary, interpretation tailored to Korean Protestant customers, a connected Christian history narrative across both countries, the Geghard vocal experience, nature and culture, and direct local operation. The final retail price will be set once airfare and partner terms are confirmed.

The price range is a provisional benchmark based on recent Korean pilgrimage and Caucasus tour prices and will be reconfirmed before actual sales.

5. Partner Strategy and Sales Structure

5.1 Role of Partner Travel Agencies

Partner travel agencies handle travel contracts and payments, while the Korea side focuses on finding customers and group leaders and proposing products.

Customer acquisition will not be left entirely to partners. In Korea, Christian networks, Korean-language product pages, social media and direct sales will be used to find potential customers and group leaders, who will then be connected to a partner travel agency at the contracting stage.

5.2 Partner Selection by Product

For Christian Heritage, priority will be given to travel agencies with pilgrimage sales experience, an existing Christian customer base, church/pastor/Christian organisation networks, and salespeople who can actually form groups.

For Boutique Journey, priority will be given to agencies with family, private group, institutional or small themed-travel customers that can present new-destination products to real customers.

Actual customer base and ability to generate bookings matter more than partner size or brand recognition.

5.3 Multiple-Partner Model

The business will not depend on one partner for all sales. Multiple channels will be developed based on product/customer fit and each partner’s strengths.

MetricDefinition
EnquiriesNumber of real customer enquiries
QuotationsNumber of detailed itinerary/price discussions
Confirmed bookingsGroups with deposit or contract completed
DeparturesActual groups and participant numbers
Repeat salesWhether the same partner sells again

Partner performance will be judged by actual enquiries, quotations, bookings and departures rather than the number or size of partnerships.

5.4 Existing MIKO Partnership

The relationship with MIKO will be assessed based on actual willingness and ability to sell. We will clarify which products they intend to sell, sales resources available, target customers and actual sales plan, and reconsider the scope of cooperation if no bookings are generated.

5.5 Minimum Group Size and Contract Principles

If minimum group size is not met, the treatment of additional cost, whether the departure proceeds, and responsibility for local supplier deposits/cancellation costs will be clearly defined in the partner contract. Before contracting, profitability by group size and key supplier cancellation terms will be checked and reflected in pricing and contract conditions.

Products are jointly developed by the Korea lead and local tour manager, taking into account both Korean market needs and local operating feasibility.

The local team checks actual hotel, transport, guide, restaurant, entrance and programme costs. In Korea, competing products and customer price acceptance are reviewed before the final supply price is set.

7.3 Standardising Operating Capability

For each product, itinerary, accommodation, transport, guides, restaurants, key programmes, suppliers, customer requests, alternative plans, actual COGS, customer feedback and improvement points will be managed in one operating document.

Interpretation quality is particularly important for Christian Heritage, so common interpretation materials will be prepared covering St. Nino, Gregory the Illuminator, the Orthodox Church, the Armenian Apostolic Church and the meaning of major monasteries and churches.

7.4 Building Local Service Capability for Korean Customers

From the first Korean groups onward, the following will be accumulated as operating data.

  • Daily driving time
  • Touring and rest time
  • Meal composition
  • Walking difficulty
  • Room and bathroom standards
  • Toilet accessibility
  • Interpretation length and style
  • Group leader requirements
  • Information that must be communicated before travel

The objective is not simply to provide Korean language support, but to build a local operating capability that genuinely satisfies Korean customers.

7.5 Scale and Growth Criteria

Organisation size and number of departures will not in themselves define business growth. The priority is to operate 3 paid groups in 2027 and 5 in 2028 reliably while confirming repeatability of products and partners.

If multiple groups overlap, bookings will be managed according to actual availability of guides, vehicles and hotels. The business will not increase departures by lowering service standards.

8. BAM Model

8.1 BAM in the Korea Tour Business

The Korea tour business considers the growth of local team members alongside the growth of the business.

Rather than presenting BAM as a separate programme, it will be reflected in how staff and guides are treated, education and development support, relationships with local suppliers and day-to-day business operations.

Customers should receive competitive products and responsible service, while local team members should receive meaningful work and opportunities to grow. Profitability is also an important foundation for sustainable employment, training and new opportunities.

8.2 Development of Staff and Guides

Korean-language training for local guides Nutsa and Qeto is intended not only to improve service for Korean customers but also to strengthen their language skills and widen future career opportunities in tourism and service.

Development is not limited to skills needed for the current job. We also consider how staff and guides want to grow and which capabilities can support their own lives and careers.

  • Understanding Korean customers
  • Service capability
  • Interpretation skills
  • Communication
  • Leadership
  • Personal career development

8.3 Ongoing Relationships and the BAM Team

Growth is not created through training programmes alone. The BAM team values ongoing relationships with local staff and guides as well as learning.

BAM team members currently support Nutsa and Qeto’s Korean study and maintain personal relationships through regular online meetings and fellowship. These relationships will continue so that the team can understand each person’s goals and, where useful, support learning and career development.

8.4 Local Economic Impact

Hotels, transport providers, restaurants, wineries and local businesses will not be selected on price alone. Service quality, trust and long-term partnership potential will also be considered.

A large portion of tour operating spend is paid to local hotels, transport, guides, restaurants and programme suppliers. Based on the current departure plan, local service purchases are approximately USD 52,840 in 2027 and USD 87,060 in 2028.

These figures show the scale of business activity that flows into the revenue of local guides, transport providers, accommodation, restaurants and programme suppliers. Actual spending will be monitored as operations begin.

10.5 Cash Flow and Financial Management

Cash flow will be recalculated monthly once other Korea operating costs are finalised, incorporating the three salary scenarios, timing of customer deposits, interim/final payments and local supplier payments.

In the early stage, annual profit and loss will be managed together with confirmed bookings, profitability by departure and monthly cash balance. Because salary level and operating cost structure directly affect break-even timing, the cost base will be adjusted based on actual 2027 operating scale and revenue conversion.
